The Corredor control plane

The product owns meaning. The model owns expression.

María wants to send $900 to her mom in Oaxaca over WhatsApp. It's her first transfer, it crosses a regulatory threshold, and a fluent AI could easily promise something the company can't keep. Follow her message through the layer that sits between the business and the model: the experience control plane.

Three layers, three owners

Designed

Intent

What María and the product are trying to do. Designers map it into flows before anything is generated.

  • Send money today
  • Required and optional moments
Governed

Commitments

Facts, disclosures and permissions that must hold on every turn. Product, legal and engineering own them.

  • ID check above $500
  • Delays must be disclosed
  • Confirm only when authorized
Generated

Expression

The actual words and layout. The model has freedom here, inside a contract.

  • Warm, plain Mexican Spanish
  • WhatsApp text, voice, or UI

Notes from building this

Where the demo could go next

The repo is deliberately small, and its README names what it leaves out. Walking through it as designers, four ideas came up that would make the control plane even easier to see.

FlowDesign the review momentAt $1,200 the runtime correctly keeps Confirm locked, but the flow has no moment that tells María a person will review the transfer. A “human review” step with its own contract would close that loop.
ContractsLet every artifact drive the runThe renderer, locale and action contracts are clearly specified but not yet read at runtime. Today the right behavior comes from flow order and the authority check. Wiring them in would make “architecture you can express in code” visible end to end.
JevCheck meaning, not keywordsThe keyword checks are a clear teaching device, but they can pass a sentence that says verification isn't required (step 6). A System One verifier in that slot, and in the intent slot, would show where Jev fits in the runtime.
ReplayRecord why words were replacedWhen the off-script model is caught, the saved record lists the replacement's passing checks rather than the original's four failures. Logging both would let the replay tell the whole story.
